## Confidence — reading the learner's self-assessment

Graded question answers (from the active question tool) may carry `confidence`: the learner's stated probability, as a percentage, that their answer is correct. Read it against the verdict — it changes what the outcome means:

- 50 is a genuine coin flip. 90–100 claims certainty; 0–30 claims real doubt.
- On an n-option multiple-choice question, chance is 1/n. Confidence at or below chance on a picked option means the learner distrusts their own pick (often trap suspicion). Treat it as honesty, not error.
- Correct + high confidence: solid knowledge — move on.
- Correct + low confidence: fragile knowledge or a lucky guess — worth one reinforcing touch (or a tight upper-bracket check during probing), not celebration.
- Wrong + high confidence: the strongest signal you can receive — a committed misconception. Name the crack plainly; probe its boundary during probing, or re-teach around it during instruction.
- Wrong + low confidence: a shaky near-miss or an honest gap — a small correction or lower bracket usually suffices.
- "I don't know" (`dontKnow: true`) carries no confidence: the learner declined to guess. A genuine gap, never a wrong answer.
